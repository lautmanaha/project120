What worked

  • Likud. Most pollsters measured Likud below the result, some by 4-6 points. The model's adjustment - halfway between the historically accurate pollsters and the rest, plus the polls' historical Likud miss - raised it from 23 seats in the poll average to 26. Actual: 27.
  • Most other lists - Yashar, Beyachad, Yisrael Beitenu, Otzma Yehudit, Religious Zionism and United Torah Judaism - landed within one seat of the forecast.
  • Overall the forecast missed by 12 seats in total, against 18 for the simple poll average; in vote share, 0.82 points per list on average against 0.98.

What missed

  • Shas - over-estimated because of the previous elections. In all five previous elections (2019-2022) the polls under-measured Shas, so the model added about 1.6 points. This time the polls over-estimated Shas (6.2% vs. 5.3% actual), and the correction doubled the error: 10 seats forecast, 6 actual. This is the largest miss, and alone accounts for most of the right-bloc gap.
  • Record, unexpected turnout in the Arab sector. Turnout in Arab localities reached about 80%, against about 53% in 2022 - a historic high, and above turnout in Jewish localities. No poll anticipated it: the Joint List and Ra'am won 14.7% together, against 11.2% in the poll average, and 19 seats instead of 15. Ra'am won 8, above its published range (5-7).
  • The Democrats. Here too the historical correction (polls used to over-estimate the left) lowered them too much: 7 forecast, 8 actual, while the poll average gave 9.

What we learned

  • Apply a historical correction only when its direction is stable. When a party's polling error flips sign between elections, widen its uncertainty instead of shifting the forecast.
  • Shared uncertainty for Arab-sector turnout. Sector turnout moves the Joint List and Ra'am together and should enter the model as one factor, with wider ranges.
  • Small lists. A list that withdraws should leave the model automatically, and lists polling below the threshold tend to lose votes at the last minute (Amcha Yisrael: 1.7% actual vs. 2.8% in the polls).
